## 1. Verdict

The plan is honest, well-scoped and unusually careful about test discipline; its core reframing (B² = soft BCE + a pairwise spread term, so the only possible gain is in the predicted vote distribution) is correct and I re-derived it. Executed as written, it would most likely land in its own outcome B: CIFAR-10H shows a per-sample spread helps, the CVS datasets show a tie with Global-S, and the clinical use case (U1) shows no gain. **At MedIA that is a borderline paper**: a methods journal in medical imaging will ask why the medical evidence is null and the positive evidence comes from 32×32 CIFAR images; novelty is thin once the classical overdispersion literature and recent label-distribution work are cited. **At TMLR it is accept-worthy** if the maths corrections below are made, because TMLR judges correctness and evidence, not novelty.

Three biggest risks:
1. **The medical half of the paper is predicted null, partly on a wrong premise.** The plan says S barely affects decisions at n = 3. That holds only at threshold 0.5. The plan's own H1 moves thresholds to about π (0.15 to 0.3), and there S moves the decision boundary in m from 0.216 (S = 1) to 0.287 (S = ∞) at t = 0.2 (my check, §3.1). The CVS use case should be rebuilt around the threshold the plan actually uses. A medical dataset with n > 3 (MHIST, 7 pathologists, binary) would also give MedIA a medical many-rater result.
2. **"Per-sample spread" may be only "spread as a function of the mean".** No baseline has S depend on m alone (S = g(m), fitted on validation). Without one, a B²-r win over Global-S cannot be read as information about x beyond m. A frozen MLP head can also memorise training images (Step 1 already saw S rise every epoch with a *frozen* encoder), so the "look-alike" meaning of S under a frozen encoder is assumed, not shown.
3. **Several predictions cannot fail or cannot pass at these sample sizes.** "CI includes 0" is used as evidence of no effect (H6a). ±1.5 pp equivalence on 40 test videos may be inconclusive, and the plan has no "inconclusive" outcome. H5's held-out spread head runs only where the predicted effect is about 0. The n = 1 point of H4 is zero by construction. The vote-allocation simulation compares against a straw-man baseline and leaks the reference votes.

## 3. Detailed notes and maths checks

All checks were run with scipy (`betabinom`, `beta`, `digamma`) in a scratch shell.

### 3.1 The "S does not affect decisions at n = 3" premise holds only at t = 0.5 (POS, U1, H0, H6) — **major**

Checked and correct in the plan:
- P(split, n = 3) = 1 − E[θ³] − E[(1−θ)³] = 3E[θ(1−θ)] = 3m(1−m)·S/(S+1) (matches `betabinom` to 1e-16).
- P_maj at m = 0.3: n = 3: 0.286 (S = 1) → 0.216 (S = ∞); n = 11: 0.277 → 0.078; n = 51: 0.273 → 0.0014. P_maj is monotone decreasing in S for m < 0.5 at all three n. As S → 0 (now allowed, since the floor is gone), P_maj → m = 0.3 for every n.
- P_maj > 0.5 ⟺ m > 0.5 for all S (symmetry at m = 0.5).

What the plan misses: H1 replaces t = 0.5 by the validation-BACC threshold, which sits near the majority prevalence π ≈ 0.15–0.3 for CVS. At those thresholds the boundary in m depends strongly on S, even at n = 3:

| threshold t on P_maj (n = 3) | m at the boundary for S = 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 5 / ∞ |
|---|---|
| 0.15 | 0.156 / 0.165 / 0.182 / 0.207 / 0.244 |
| 0.20 | 0.206 / 0.216 / 0.232 / 0.255 / 0.287 |
| 0.30 | 0.306 / 0.313 / 0.326 / 0.342 / 0.363 |

So a frame with m = 0.25 is "criterion met" if the model says S = 2 and "not met" if it says S = ∞, at t = 0.2. Consequences:
- Contribution 1(b) and the §2 sentence "for 3-rater CVS data the spread … barely affects the majority decision" must be qualified to t = 0.5.
- **H0 is ambiguous** until the plan says which score is thresholded. If B²-r thresholds P_maj and soft BCE thresholds m, the BACC comparison includes an S effect. Recommendation: m for all arms (primary; tests the mean claim cleanly), P_maj for B²-r and Global-S as secondary (tests whether S helps at the operating threshold).
- **This opens a better CVS use case than U1 as written.** A CVS assistant must decide "confirm" at a low threshold (missing a criterion is cheap, false confirmation is not). Whether S shifts the right frames across that boundary is testable at n = 3: compare BACC and false-confirmation rate at the validation threshold for P_maj(B²-r) against P_maj(Global-S) and against m. This is cheap (post-hoc) and is the most likely place for a non-null CVS result.

### 3.2 H1: the "impossible with a frozen encoder" claim (H1) — minor

BACC = ½(TPR + TNR); pointwise, δ(x) = 1 iff η(x)/π > (1−η(x))/(1−π) iff η > π. Correct, and under pure label shift this rule is unchanged (it equals a likelihood-ratio rule), so BACC thresholds transfer across the Endoscapes val/test shift. Weighting with w₊/w₋ = N₋/N₊ = (1−π)/π moves the hard-BCE 0.5 threshold to η > π: correct. But "the two routes can differ only when the weights change features, impossible with a frozen encoder" is wrong. The head has a 256-unit hidden layer, and with any finite capacity or misspecification, weighted and unweighted ERM converge to different functions. For soft BCE or B² with majority-label weights, the weighted optimum is not even a monotone transform of η (it depends on the conditional law of k, 01_foundations §5). The prediction can stand; the justification should say "expected small, measured".

H1(b): the weighted arm inflates P_maj by design (foundations §5: ×3.3 at m = 0.15), so "ECE falls ≥ 40%" cannot realistically fail. "Mean θ on test within ±0.03 of the observed test vote fraction" can fail because of the documented val/test prevalence shift (C1 majority prevalence 0.163 → 0.235), not because of miscalibration. Compare with the validation vote fraction instead.

### 3.3 H2: stability and the floor diagnostic — minor, but the diagnostic must change

With α₁ = softplus(f) + ε, for k ≥ 1: ∂L/∂α₁ ≈ −1/α₁ (from ψ(α₁) − ψ(k+α₁)). For k = 0 the 1/α₁ terms cancel (∂L/∂α₁ = ψ(n+S) − ψ(S) ≈ 1.08). Chain rule: softplus′(f) = σ(f) ≈ softplus(f) as f → −∞, so |∂L/∂f| ≲ 1:

| f | α₁ | ∂L/∂α₁ (k = 1) | ∂L/∂f (k = 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| −5 | 7.7e-3 | −128 | −0.86 |
| −7 | 1.9e-3 | −522 | −0.48 |
| −10 | 1.05e-3 | −956 | −0.043 |
| −15 | 1.0e-3 | −999 | −3e-4 |

So there is **no explosion** (the plan's "≤ 10³" bound is on the wrong variable). The real risk is **saturation**: once a logit is pushed far negative, a later k ≥ 1 sample can barely pull it back. That is a new, smaller floor-sticking effect at ε. The fallback ladder (ε = 0.1, then the mean–precision head) covers it.

Prediction (a) counts min(α) < ε + 0.05 as "on the floor". Legitimate CVS Betas do not satisfy that test: m = 0.02–0.05 with S = 1–2 gives α₁ = 0.02–0.05 (checked). The diagnostic would flag correct predictions. Measure saturation directly: the share of pre-softplus logits below −7 (α within 2ε).

The "data ask for α < 1" argument uses the moment-matched **marginal** S (2.3/1.0/1.5). The conditional S given x is larger than the marginal one, because the mean varies with x. The empirical evidence (constant S ≈ 1–2 beats predicted S; 20–36% of C2/C3 on the floor) is the stronger argument; lead with it.

### 3.4 H3: post-hoc c — consistency fine, fairness not specified

max over c of Σ log BB(k | n, cα₁, cα₀) on validation is a one-parameter M-estimator of a strictly proper score, so it is consistent for the best c in the family. Correct. At n_val = 1 the likelihood is exactly flat in c (checked: identical NLL for c = 0.1 … 10⁴), which matters for H4. The comparison in H3(b) is fair only if the corrected-KL arm also receives c. Otherwise "no-KL + c" beats "KL" partly because it is calibrated. The same applies to the +floor arm in H2, where c < 1 partly undoes the floor (c·α < 1 becomes possible). Pre-specify: every B² arm gets c. Report the trap effects (the "level" damage) without c as a secondary row.

### 3.5 H4: n = 1 behaviour, nesting, and what "per-sample" means — **major**

- **Nesting.** The B²-r function class (any m(x), S(x), then ×c) contains (m_soft(x), S*). So the population BB-NLL optimum of B²-r is never worse than Global-S. Correct, population-only, as the plan says.
- **n = 1.** p(k = 1) = α₁/(α₁+α₀) = m, so the loss constrains only m. In the (f₁, f₀) parametrisation the gradient is not orthogonal to the S level sets, so S **drifts** with the optimiser, weight decay and initialisation. It does not "stay at initialisation". c and S* are unidentified (flat validation likelihood). Unless a tie-break is fixed in advance (c = S* = ∞ → both arms become Binomial(m), and B²-r's m equals soft BCE's at n = 1), the full-panel test NLL at n_train = 1 is arbitrary. With the tie-break, the zero margin is true by construction. Either way, n_train = 1 is an anchor, not a test of H4(b). The growth test should use n_train ∈ {3, 5, 10, all}.
- **Per-sample vs per-mean.** Because P(split), P_maj and P(θ > 0.5) are functions of (m, S), a spread that depends only on m adds nothing beyond the mean. Step 1 found S tracking m strongly. B²-r beating Global-S therefore does not show that α carries information about x beyond m. **Add the S = g(m) baseline** (binned or isotonic on validation). Make "B²-r beats S = g(m) on full-panel vote NLL" the per-sample claim, and "B²-r or S = g(m) beats Global-S" the spread-varies claim.
- **Dilution.** For one-vs-rest heads most pairs have k_c ≈ 0 of ~51. The +0.02 nats/image criterion averages over them. Pre-specify an ambiguous subset (e.g. images whose full-panel plurality share is < 0.9) and report H4 there as well. Report NLL per vote, since n varies 47–63.

### 3.6 Hypergeometric "3 random raters" target (U2b, MET) — correct, wording off

For an image with N votes, K for class c, the share of 3-subsets that split, 1 − [C(K,3) + C(N−K,3)]/C(N,3), is a U-statistic. Its expectation given θ, with K ~ Bin(N, θ), is exactly 3θ(1−θ) (checked at θ = 0.05/0.2/0.5, N = 51). It is an **unbiased estimate**, not the "exact value". MSE against it = MSE against 3θ(1−θ) + a noise term common to all arms. Arm comparisons by paired differences are valid; absolute MSEs include an irreducible floor (report it, e.g. by jackknife). CIFAR-10H raters are a different random subset of 2,571 workers per image, so the "random rater" reading holds about as well as anywhere. No further assumption work is needed.

### 3.7 CIFAR-10H one-vs-rest heads and the fixed model — fine

If each image's votes are iid Categorical(p), then k_c | p ~ Bin(n, p_c) exactly. The Beta on θ_c is the same working assumption as for CVS. The ten heads are not jointly coherent (Σ_c m_c ≠ 1; ten Betas are not a Dirichlet). That is harmless for per-head claims, but the paper must not compute multiclass quantities from them. Also, "majority positive" for head c is "more than half chose c", which differs from the CIFAR plurality label. State it.

### 3.8 Two-level bootstrap with unpaired seeds (STAT) — valid, under-powered on the seed axis

Resampling test clusters jointly for both arms (paired on test data) and seeds independently per arm targets "a new run of A versus a new run of B on a new test set". It is conservative when seeds share data order or initialisation. Issues: with 5 seeds the bootstrap seed variance is biased by 4/5 and very noisy. In the frozen regime a seed costs 3 min, so use 10 seeds (equivalence tests on 40 videos need it). Specify the statistic: the metric per seed averaged over resampled seeds, not the metric of averaged predictions (that is the ensemble). For ensembles, the resampled ensemble is the mixture over the resampled member set. Validation-fitted quantities (threshold, S*, c) are not resampled; say so (acceptable).

### 3.9 Refutability and margins (H5, H6, SUCC, OUTC) — **major**

- **H6(a)** "AURC difference < 0.005 with CI including 0" passes whenever the CI is wide, which is likely with 40 videos. Either make it equivalence (CI inside ±δ, δ from the pilot) or drop it.
- **H5(c)** orders three margins that are each predicted ≤ 0.01 on CVS. With clustered CIs this ordering is noise. The held-out spread head's "recovers ≥ half the frozen margin" is half of about 0. Move the decisive test to CIFAR-10H, where the frozen margin is predicted ≥ 0.02 nats.
- **H5(b)** "frozen S flat within ±20% after epoch 3" is contradicted by Step 1 (frozen SwinV2: val S 2.5 → 5.1 over 7 epochs; with the floor and weights on, but the mechanism, a head isolating training points, does not need them). A 768→256 MLP trained 50 epochs on ~7k strongly correlated frames (5 s apart within 120 videos) can memorise. Cheapest check: frozen regime with (i) a linear head and (ii) a 5-fold **cross-fitted S-head** (S fitted on folds the mean head did not see). If cross-fitting changes the B²-r–Global-S margin, frozen S was partly memorisation. A few minutes of compute.
- **No inconclusive category** anywhere (H0, S0–S6, §4.8). Add one: CI straddles the margin → "inconclusive", which maps to the weaker story.
- **Margins are set before knowing CI widths.** Run a validation-only pilot in Round 0 (tuning-grid seeds, two-level bootstrap on val) and set every margin from it before freezing §4.7. This is legitimate because it uses no test data.

### 3.10 Model selection (SEL) — revise

Every arm is selected on validation mAP, but the primary endpoints are vote-level. Step 1 shows S changes every epoch, so the mAP-selected epoch sets S, and c can fix its level but not its ranking. Since frozen runs are minutes, save predictions at every epoch. Pre-specify two checkpoints per run, each chosen by the same rule for all arms: best val mAP (Table 1) and best val vote NLL after the post-hoc spread step (Tables 2–3; for soft BCE that is after S*). Tuning on 1 seed with 6 grid points per family is equal across families: acceptable.

### 3.11 Over-theorising (user's instruction) — where the plan can save effort

- **Annotator dependence is handled proportionately.** The Endoscapes per-rater table plus one limitation paragraph is enough. The sequential marking can push S up (under-dispersion), which could change a conclusion only for "S on Endoscapes is informative". The plan already requires CIFAR-10H for that claim. No more work needed. But do not sell Endoscapes vs CVS Challenge as a test of panel structure: the datasets differ in institutions, format and prevalence, so a difference cannot be attributed to the panel.
- **Point predictions with no basis**: c ∈ [0.7, 1.4], LoRA c < 0.7, full fine-tuning c < 0.5, the ≥ 40% ECE drop, ≥ 0.02 nats in H2. These are guesses dressed as theory. Keep them as descriptive expectations and freeze only the sign-and-margin criteria.
- **Contribution 1(c)** (Fisher information vs n) is textbook for the Beta-Binomial intra-class correlation. One figure and a citation, not a contribution.
- **Fallback ladder, F2 head, B²-M prior-shift readout.** All fine and cheap; no further theory needed.
- **Not over-theorised enough** in one place: the meaning of S under a frozen encoder rests on an untested premise (3.9). The cross-fit check is the cheapest adequate fix.

## 4. What a reviewer would demand that the plan does not have

1. **A direct disagreement predictor as a baseline** (Raghu et al., ICML 2019, verified: "direct uncertainty prediction" of doctor disagreement beats deriving it from a classifier). Cheap version: soft BCE head plus a second sigmoid head trained by BCE on the split indicator 1[k ∈ {1, …, n−1}] (CVS) or on the hypergeometric 3-rater split (CIFAR-10H). Frozen regime, minutes. Without it, U1/U2 reviewers will say the Beta is an indirect route to a target that can be learned directly.
2. **Mean-conditional spread (S = g(m)) baseline** (§3.5). This is the test of "per-sample".
3. **A medical dataset with more than 3 raters**, ideally to give MedIA a non-null medical result. **MHIST** (Wei et al., AIME 2021; verified): 3,152 colorectal-polyp H&E tiles (224×224), binary HP vs SSA, **7 GI pathologists per image**, official 2,175/977 split, free with a data-use form. k_SSA follows from the majority label and the agreement level (a/7 → k = a or 7 − a). Whether the CSV gives the SSA count directly, and whether the split is patient-grouped, is UNVERIFIED. It fits the fixed model with no new formulation, allows n_train ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}, and costs about half a day of loader work plus frozen runs. Optional alternative with variable n (1–4 raters): LIDC-IDRI malignancy ratings, but that needs 3-D crops and is heavier.
4. **A calibration metric for the second-order output itself.** Ahdritz et al. 2024 (verified; "higher-order calibration" measured with k-snapshots) give exactly the right notion: the predicted mixture must match the empirical label variability among points that receive the same prediction. That is the plan's own "spread among look-alikes" reading of S. Report a binned version (predicted vs observed P(split), predicted vs observed vote pmf), with n = 3 snapshots on CVS and full panels on CIFAR-10H.
5. **Classical overdispersion citations** (Beta-Binomial for clustered binary data; intra-class correlation ρ = 1/(S+1); identification from within-cluster pairs). TMLR reviewers will spot the gap.
6. **Disclosure that the Endoscapes test set informed the hypotheses** (Step 1 analysed it).
7. **A one-paragraph justification for frame-level-only evaluation.** The MICCAI reviewers asked for video-level baselines. The user has fixed "no per-video evaluation". The paper should say why (scope: panel prediction per frame) so the omission reads as a decision, not a gap.
8. **Power/CI-width pilot** before margins are frozen (§3.9).
9. **Clinical asymmetry**: the error that matters for CVS is false confirmation. One pre-specified asymmetric endpoint (false-confirmation rate at fixed coverage among confirmed frames) is more convincing to MedIA readers than symmetric AURC.

**Is what remains novel enough?** What survives: (i) the diagnosis of three implementation traps in second-order heads trained on vote counts, with measured effects; (ii) a controlled real-data identifiability study of a *per-sample* spread against n (distinct from first-order saturation studies); (iii) the encoder-regime analysis (if H5 is redesigned so that it can fail); (iv) a CVS application with matched baselines. For **TMLR** this is sufficient, since novelty is not the criterion and the negative or nuanced findings are useful. For **MedIA** it is borderline. The methods novelty is modest and the medical results are predicted null. The cheapest way to cross the bar is the §3.1 threshold-level CVS analysis plus a medical many-rater dataset (MHIST).
