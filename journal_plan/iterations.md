## CHECKPOINT 2026-10-20 — review of round D so far (user asked to keep this for later)

**Status.** Nothing finalised. B²-r stays the pre-registered method (tag `prereg-b2r-2026-10-16`); no round-D change beats it consistently. D1 B²-ms mixed (better CIFAR-10H, MHIST; worse Endoscapes, AMi-Br, FER+); D2 invalid; D2b dropped; D3 seed ensembles kept as a reported configuration (B² lead survives ensembling); D0b: the per-image spread is informative beyond m with ≥ ~7 votes per image, noise with 3.

**1. Can B² recover the hidden mean better than BCE from few votes?** With unlimited images, no: soft BCE with target k/n is minimised exactly at E[k/n | x] = θ(x) for any n; B²'s m is the mean of the KL-closest Beta-Binomial, equal to θ(x) only if votes are exactly Beta-Binomial (exchangeable raters). With finite images B² can win through one mechanism, weighting: it treats an image's n votes as n/(1 + (n − 1)ρ) independent votes (ρ = 1/(S + 1)) — quasi-likelihood weighting for overdispersed binomial data (Williams 1982), more efficient than equal weights when the weights are right. With the same n and the same ρ for every image the weights are equal and B² cannot gain on m. Gains need (a) n varying across images, or (b) ρ varying across images and learnable. Predictions: 3 fixed votes (Endoscapes, AMi-Br) → no gain (observed); FER+, the only dataset with variable n (4–10), is the only one where B²-r improved discrimination (mAP +0.010) — our soft BCE weights 4- and 10-vote faces equally.

**2. Why the spread is hard to learn and to see.** (i) Yardstick: even an oracle per-image S (half the images S = 1, half S = 100, same m) beats the best single S by only ≈ 5% vote NLL at n = 3, ≈ 10% at 7, 15–22% at 50; with S = 3 vs 30: ≈ 1%, 3%, 6–7%. At 3 votes a perfect spread is nearly invisible. (ii) Learning: per training image the B² loss is minimised by m = k/n and S → ∞ for split panels (S irrelevant for unanimous ones), the opposite of the generalising pattern; S becomes meaningful only through the network failing to fit individual images (pooling over similar images) — matches the early rise of validation NLL. Joint mean–variance learning degrading the mean is known for Gaussian NLL (Seitzer et al., ICLR 2022, β-NLL; Stirn et al., AISTATS 2023); not documented for count likelihoods. (iii) Real data vs the model: fixed raters with systematic biases break exchangeability (Endoscapes "spread" is mostly rater bias); CIFAR-10H is 85% unanimous image–class pairs; frozen DINOv3 features may encode ambiguity only weakly.

**3. Architecture.** Separate per-label MLP heads vs one merged head (3×2 vs 1×6): merging only shares the hidden layer, a mild regulariser; the likelihood is unchanged → not expected to matter. The real mismatch is the likelihood on multi-class data (CIFAR-10H, FER+): K independent one-vs-rest Beta-Binomials, each with its own S from a weak binary signal, means not summing to 1. The matching model is the Dirichlet-Multinomial (one mean vector and one concentration per image). Established prior art: Walmsley et al., MNRAS 2022 (Galaxy Zoo DECaLS); Wang et al., arXiv:2609.17130 (Sept 2026, facial expression video, 10 votes). User (2026-10-20): a full pivot to it would be less novel; wants to read more first.

**4. B²-ms** is a reparameterisation of the same model (same distributions, same optimum); differences are optimisation-only (gradient geometry, what weight decay pulls towards). No theoretical case → dropped as a candidate.

**5. Ranked next steps (as proposed):** (1) mean recovery from few votes on existing runs [D5a]; (2) vote-count-weighted soft BCE on the variable-n datasets [D5b]; (3) Dirichlet-Multinomial head for multi-class data; (4) synthetic study of when B² beats BCE on m and on S; (5) Galaxy Zoo DECaLS (many, variable votes). Balanced accuracy is equal (0.931 vs 0.930), so part of the accuracy gap is the threshold.

## D7 — synthetic study: when does B² beat soft BCE, on the mean and on the spread? (planned 2026-10-21, before any run)
- **Aim:** a world with known truth, to map the regimes the checkpoint theory describes, and to test the user's few-votes hypothesis where the hidden mean is known exactly.
- **Generator (adapted from the user's earlier test-bench).** Kept from it: a latent concept u ~ N(0, I_16); features z = U u + ε (D = 768, the DINOv3 ViT-B width; σ_z = 0.7), so the model sees a noisy high-dimensional view; a score direction a and an orthogonal clarity direction d; calibration of the vote histogram to a real dataset; an oracle reference. Changed, and why: in the old generator θ = sigmoid(η/τ) is a deterministic function of u, so given the features the votes are (nearly) Binomial — soft BCE is then the correctly specified model and B²'s spread has nothing to learn; its overdispersion option uses one ρ for all frames, which the Global-S step already captures. Our theory says B² can only help when the overdispersion *varies across images and is predictable from the features*, or when n varies. So here clarity drives the per-image spread: mean μ(u) = sigmoid((aᵀu + b)/τ); spread log S(u) = log S̄ + γ · dᵀu (γ = 0: one spread for all images); each image's true vote share θ_i ~ Beta(μ S, (1 − μ) S) (the part of θ the features do not explain); votes k ~ Binomial(n_i, θ_i).
- **Misspecified worlds:** (i) logit-normal instead of Beta (θ_i = sigmoid(logit μ + σ(u) ε), σ driven by clarity); (ii) a fixed panel of 3 raters with personal biases β_r (vote_r ~ Bernoulli(sigmoid(logit θ_i + β_r))), Endoscapes-like.
- **Training:** the real trainer (`train.run`: same MLP head, AdamW lr 3e-4 / wd 1e-2, 15 epochs, checkpoints A/B on a validation set that has the same few votes, spread step on validation), single binary label, 5 seeds (each seed redraws the world's data and the init).
- **Scored against the truth** on 5,000 test images: mean — MSE(m, μ(u)) (the best any model can do from features) and MSE(m, θ_i); spread — Spearman(predicted S, true S) and the gap to the oracle (true μ, S) in vote NLL on fresh panels of 3, 10 and 50 votes; discrimination — AUROC of m for θ_i > 0.5. References: oracle (true μ, S); soft BCE trained on θ_i itself (the best possible mean from features with infinite votes).
- **Sweeps (one factor at a time around a base world: n = 3, 6,000 training images, γ strong, Beta world):**
  - S1 votes per image n ∈ {1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50}, γ ∈ {0, strong} — the user hypothesis and spread learnability;
  - S2 training images ∈ {1,500, 6,000, 24,000} at n ∈ {3, 10};
  - S3 variable n (uniform 3–20; FER+-like 4–10) vs fixed n with the same mean, γ ∈ {0, strong}; adds `soft_bce_n` (tests the weighting account);
  - S4 misspecification at n = 3: Beta, logit-normal, biased fixed raters.
  Models: soft BCE, B²-r (+ `soft_bce_n` in S3). ≈ 300 CPU runs (array job).
- **Predictions (from the checkpoint theory):** no mean gain at fixed n with γ = 0; a mean gain only with variable n or (γ > 0, many images, moderate n); the spread learnable (Spearman > 0) only at moderate-to-large n with many images; with biased raters the learned spread tracks rater bias, not images. If B² improves the mean at n = 3 and fixed n with γ = 0, the theory is wrong.
- **Outcome (2026-10-22; job 7575104, 445/445 runs ok, + report 7575105; `results/journal/synth/synth_report.md`; scored against the known truth, 5 seeds):**
  - **Mean (MSE to μ):** no reliable B² gain in any world. Per-setting Δ is ≤ about 1 sd and seeds split 3/5 or 4/5 in most rows. At checkpoint B every fixed-n γ = 0 row has a positive Δ (B²-r 0.3–0.5 × 10⁻³ closer, ≈ 5% of MSE). But B²-r with 20–50 votes also beats the 200-vote soft-BCE reference (share recovered > 100%), so this looks like a training-dynamics difference between the losses, not information from the votes. Prediction "no mean gain at fixed n, γ = 0": held in size, with that small systematic sign. Prediction "mean gain with variable n": not supported (S3: soft BCE vote-weighted ≈ soft BCE; B² Δ inconsistent). The user's few-votes hypothesis is not supported here either (as D5a on real data).
  - **Spread:** learnable from few votes when it really varies. Spearman(S, true clarity) is 0.66 at n = 3, rising to 0.82 at n = 50 (6,000 images); 0.47 with 1,500 images; ≈ 0 when γ = 0, as it should be. Prediction "only at moderate-to-large n" WRONG: 3 votes per image suffice in this world.
  - **Panel prediction (excess vote NLL over the oracle, Global-S → B²-r), when spread varies (γ > 0):** a large gain that grows with the size of the panel predicted. Trained on 3 votes: 0.050 → 0.043 (3-vote panels), 0.118 → 0.087 (10), 0.225 → 0.154 (50 votes, −31%). Trained on 50 votes: 0.211 → 0.115 on 50-vote panels (−45%). It holds under misspecification (logit-normal world, n = 3: 0.177 → 0.108 at 3 votes; 0.575 → 0.320 at 50). When the spread is constant (γ = 0), B²-r pays a small price for estimating an unnecessary spread (50-vote panels: 0.0975 vs 0.1019).
  - **Discrimination:** AUROC equal everywhere (±0.002).
  - **Biased-rater world:** not informative as designed. The same 3 raters label every image, so rater bias is constant across images and cannot drive per-image spread. The spread still tracks clarity (0.61). The design should have used rater panels that differ by image.
  - **Reading:** B² is a model of the spread, not a better estimator of the mean. Its benefit appears in panel prediction exactly when the per-image spread varies and is predictable from the features, and it is largest when predicting larger panels than were trained on. That matches the real-data D0b split: informative spread on CIFAR-10H and MHIST, noise on Endoscapes and AMi-Br. The synthetic result says 3 votes are enough in principle, so Endoscapes' null more likely means its spread does not vary predictably (or is rater-driven) than that 3 votes are too few. Caveat: a clean 16-dim world; real features carry the spread less cleanly.

## D8 — B²-rba: B² with rater bias adjustment (planned 2026-10-22, before any run; user: "strong lead", approved)
- **Diagnosis:** on Endoscapes and AMi-Br the same 3 raters label every image, and their yes-rates differ a lot (Endoscapes C1 3% / 16% / 28%, C2 10 / 12 / 15%, C3 27 / 11 / 24%; AMi-Br atypical 14 / 23 / 29%). B²-r only sees the count k, so (yes, no, no) and (no, no, yes) are the same to it and systematic rater habits must go into the spread S. D0b found per-image S to be noise on exactly these two datasets; D7 says 3 votes suffice to learn a spread that really varies, so rater bias is the prime suspect.
- **Model (B²-rba, `b2rba`):** per image and label the head gives α₀, α₁ exactly as B²-r (softplus + ε): a typical rater's share θ ~ Beta(m S, (1 − m) S). Rater r votes yes with probability σ(logit θ + β_r); β_r is one learned number per rater and label (the head's only new parameters, R × K), constrained to sum to zero over the panel, so "typical rater" = the panel average (not "the correct rater"). Loss = −log P(the ordered votes) = −log ∫ Beta(θ) Π_r Bernoulli(y_r; σ(logit θ + β_r)) dθ, by a fixed 257-point quadrature on the logit scale (two stretched half-grids around logit m, analytic far tails, self-normalised; only log / exp on the GPU; error ≤ 5e-5 nats against the exact Beta-Binomial at β = 0, unit-tested). β is fitted by gradient descent jointly with the head (at the optimum each rater's predicted yes-rate matches their observed one, as a logistic-regression intercept). β has its own Adam step size 1e-2 and no weight decay (fixed before any run, not tuned): it is a logit-scale intercept (order 1), and Adam moves a parameter by ≈ lr per step, so at the head's lr (1e-4) it could move only ≈ 0.1 in a whole Endoscapes run (seen in a CPU toy, 2026-10-22); decay would pull the raters towards identical. In the toy, β is recovered (±1.0 for true ±1) once the mean is well fitted; with an under-fitted mean β is biased (B²-rba up, soft BCE-ba down), so β is only as good as m.
- **Control (soft BCE-ba, `soft_bce_ba`):** the same with no per-image spread: each vote ~ Bernoulli(σ(logit m + β_r)), loss = mean over the image's raters of the per-vote BCE (soft BCE is the β = 0 case); one spread per label fitted on validation afterwards (as Global-S). Together with soft BCE and B²-r this is a 2 × 2: {rater bias no / yes} × {per-image spread no / yes}.
- **Training:** frozen DINOv3 features, same MLP head, AdamW, 15 epochs, 4 evaluations per epoch, checkpoints A (val mAP of m vs the panel majority) and B (val NLL of the ordered votes after the spread step). Fairness: the two new variants get the same 6-point lr × wd grid that tuned the baselines (block `tune_ba`, 24 runs, same choice rule) → `tune_ba/chosen.json`. Main block `d8`: Endoscapes + AMi-Br × {soft BCE, B²-r, soft BCE-ba, B²-rba} × 5 seeds (40 runs).
- **Endpoints (validation only; two-level bootstrap 95% CI over videos / slides and seeds):**
  - P1 panel prediction: NLL per vote of the ORDERED votes, B²-rba − B²-r (and soft BCE-ba − soft BCE). For a model that treats raters as interchangeable every ordering of the same k is equally likely, so its ordered NLL = count NLL − log C(n, k): the yardstick is fair, and a rater-aware model can only win by knowing who voted.
  - P2 is S informative once bias is removed: within B²-rba, per-image S vs one S per label (same m and β): ΔNLL and split AUROC; against the same ablation within B²-r (D0b).
  - P3 interaction: [soft BCE-ba − B²-rba] vs [soft BCE − B²-r] ordered NLL (does the per-image spread help more after bias adjustment?).
  - Secondary (comparable with all earlier tables): count vote NLL for the known panel, P_maj ECE / Brier, split AP / AUROC / catch-20 (count pmf by enumerating the 2³ vote patterns); discrimination of m vs the majority (mAP, AUROC, BACC, F1, ACC); learned β vs each rater's observed yes-rate.
