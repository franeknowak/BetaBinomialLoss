# 03 — Scope critique of `02_proposals_r0.md` (round 0)

*Scope critic, 2026-10-08. I judged only what the plan says, against brief 01b §E and the repo at `main@2f59ef9` (read-only). I ran no experiments. "User-days" means the PhD student's working days. Claude sessions are counted as part of those days, not on top of them.*

## 1. Verdict

The scientific core is sound and fits one journal paper. It has three parts: the theory (B² = soft BCE + O(1/S)), the CIFAR-10H vote-subsampling identifiability test (H4), and an honest outcome→story table. The plan as written is about **1.5–2× too large for one student**, and its effort estimate is **about 2× too low**. Round 0 is quoted at 5 user-days. Against the repo it is 10–12 days, because `main` hard-codes 3 heads, n = 3 and Endoscapes in the model, the loss, the metrics and `main.py`. The LoRA injector exists but nothing calls it, and the embedding cache, the head-only trainer, the post-hoc fitting, the metrics and the bootstrap are all new. The plan has 7 hypotheses, 3 use cases, 9 frozen arms and a 3-regime Round 2 with a novel "held-out concentration head". That volume of results is more than the student can analyse and write up well.

Cut to **5 hypotheses** (H1–H3 merged into one "traps" ablation), **2 use cases** (U3 is cut), **8 frozen arms**, and a **small Round 2** (full fine-tuning only, no LoRA, no held-out concentration head). Take the CVS Challenge off the critical path. Remove every option whose inclusion depends on results (the synthetic check, ChaosNLI, test clips that "replace our split"). Fix the equivalence margins so they can actually be met with about 40 test videos. Realistic total: **about 20 user-days before writing** for the trimmed plan, against **about 27** for the plan as written.

## 4. Notes on the revise and cut items

**4.1 Why merge H1–H3.** These three are one finding ("three implementation traps set S instead of the data"), and each is tested by a single-factor ablation of one recipe. As three hypotheses they produce 9 sub-predictions, 3 success rows and 3 sections. As one hypothesis they produce one table (Δ against B²-r for each trap, on BACC/ECE and vote NLL) and one figure (S against m), which is also how a reader remembers them. This loses no experiments and saves a few days of writing.

**4.2 Why Round 2 shrinks most.** Round 2 is the only block where GPU time, engineering and analysis are all large, and its main hypothesis (H5) is secondary to the paper's claims. Every outcome in §4.8 of the plan is decided by Round 1 (the plan says so itself). The held-out concentration head is a new method in its own right. Under the current softplus (α₀, α₁) head, "freeze m and refit S" cannot be done without a mean–precision reparametrisation, so it silently depends on the cut F2 code. LoRA adds a third regime to every figure for a prediction ("between frozen and full") that adds little. Full fine-tuning alone gives the decisive contrast (S drifts; c ≪ 1; the margin collapses).

**4.3 The CVS Challenge.** The plan correctly flags the licence. But it leaves the dataset inside the claim rule ("2 of 3"), the success criteria and Round 2, so a "no" from the organisers would force a re-plan after pre-registration. The fix is cheap: pre-register on two datasets and treat CVS-C as confirmatory if it arrives in time. The paper then loses only the fixed-panel-against-rater-pool comparison, which is one paragraph.

**4.4 Equivalence margins.** I did not compute CI widths. But BACC over about 40 test videos, with C1/C3 prevalence near 0.2–0.28 and strong within-video correlation, typically gives video-bootstrap half-widths of several percentage points. A ±1.5 pp equivalence margin would then be unattainable whatever the truth is. This is a scope issue because an unattainable criterion either forces post-hoc rescue (iteration) or leaves H0 with no conclusion. Sizing the margin from the existing MICCAI-era test predictions takes about an hour.

**4.5 U2 and the direct-split baseline.** Raghu et al. (ICML 2019) is cited as the closest prior art for triage, and its finding is that *direct* disagreement prediction beats deriving it from a classifier. Without that baseline, a reviewer can dismiss U2. With frozen embeddings it is one more head (BCE on 1[k ∈ {1, 2}]), minutes per run and no new code. This is the only addition I recommend.

**4.6 What a minimal accept-worthy paper still needs (missing from the plan).**
- A paragraph stating what is new relative to the MICCAI paper (MedIA asks for substantial extension; state the new theory, recipe, datasets and use cases).
- A qualitative CVS figure (frames with high and low predicted P(split)), produced from the prediction files.
- Code and config release on the `journal-extension` branch, with the frozen success criteria committed and dated (the plan has the commit, not the release).
- Test silence in the trainer (SEL note). Otherwise the "test opened once" claim is not true in practice.
- Nothing else. In particular, no additional dataset, modality or baseline (beyond the direct-split head) is needed.

**4.7 Claude-usage note.** The plan's large Round 0 is where sessions are most likely to be cut mid-task. Write each module (a–f in §3.1) to a short spec file first, implement it against a unit test, and commit it before the next module starts. Each Claude session then needs to load only one spec and one module, not the whole plan.

**4.8 Counts.** Accept 10 (H4, D-ENDO, D-C10H, BL-HARD, BL-SOFT, BL-GLOBALS, SEL, OUTC, JOUR, NOV). Revise 19 (POS, U1, U2, H0, H1, H2, H3, H5, H6, D-CVSC, BL-ENS, BL-B2M, MET, STAT, R0, R1, R2, R3, SUCC). Cut 3 (U3, D-SYNTH, D-CHAOSNLI). There is also one sub-cut inside a revise item that matters more than any full cut: LoRA plus the held-out concentration head in H5/R2.
